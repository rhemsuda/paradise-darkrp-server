Registering RiftMetro Powders as a Business Entity and Obtaining an HST Number in Ontario

This guide outlines the steps to register RiftMetro Powders as a legal business entity in Ontario, Canada, and obtain an HST number for tax purposes. It’s tailored for your powder coating and tire business in New Hamburg, Ontario, and assumes you’re starting with a sole proprietorship (simplest option) but also explores incorporating as a corporation (equivalent to an LLC). Consult an accountant or lawyer for final decisions on business structure and tax compliance.

Step 1: Choose a Business Structure





Why? The business structure determines how you register, file taxes, and manage liability. In Canada, LLCs are not a standard business form; the closest equivalents are a sole proprietorship or a corporation.



Options:





Sole Proprietorship: You own and operate the business personally. Simplest to set up, but you’re personally liable for debts. Suitable for starting small and minimizing costs.



Corporation: A separate legal entity (like an LLC in the U.S.), offering limited liability protection but with higher setup costs and complexity. Recommended if you plan to scale or seek significant financing.



Recommendation for RiftMetro Powders: Start as a sole proprietorship to keep costs low while building your client base for tire services and powder coating. You can incorporate later if the business grows or you need liability protection.



Action:





Decide on sole proprietorship for now (revisit incorporation after year 1).



If choosing a corporation, consult a lawyer to file Articles of Incorporation (cost: ~$360 CAD for provincial incorporation).

Step 2: Register Your Business Name





Why? Registering “RiftMetro Powders” with the Ontario government establishes it as your official business name and links it to your Business Identification Number (BIN) for provincial purposes.



Action:





Check Name Availability: Use the Ontario Business Registry (ONBIS) online to ensure “RiftMetro Powders” is unique and not in use.



Register the Name: File a Business Name Registration (Form 1) under the Business Names Act via:





Online: Ontario Business Registry (cost: $60 CAD, immediate processing).



In-Person: ServiceOntario center.



Required: Your name, address, and business description (e.g., “Powder coating and tire services”).



Receive BIN: You’ll get a 9-digit Business Identification Number (BIN) and a company key for future filings. This is not the same as a CRA Business Number (BN).



Note: Registration lasts 5 years and can be renewed for $60 CAD. If incorporating, you’ll need an Ontario-biased NUANS name search report (~$50 CAD) instead.

Step 3: Obtain a Business Number (BN) from the CRA





Why? A Business Number (BN) is a 9-digit identifier for interacting with the CRA for taxes (e.g., HST, payroll). It’s required for HST registration and other CRA program accounts.



Action:





Register for a BN:





Online: Use the CRA’s Business Registration Online (BRO) portal (fastest method, immediate BN issuance).



Phone: Call CRA at 1-800-959-5525 for guidance.



Mail: Download and submit Form RC1 from the CRA website.



Required: Your Social Insurance Number (SIN) for sole proprietorship, business name, address, and type (sole proprietorship).



Receive BN: You’ll get a 9-digit BN (e.g., 987654321). For sole proprietorships, this is tied to your SIN.



Note: If you incorporate later, you’ll need a new BN for the corporation.

Step 4: Register for an GST/HST Account





Why? In Ontario, businesses charge 13% HST on taxable services like tire mounting and powder coating. You must register for an HST account if your annual revenue exceeds $30,000 CAD in any four consecutive quarters or if you want to claim input tax credits (ITCs) for HST paid on business expenses (e.g., equipment, materials).



When to Register:





Mandatory: If your gross revenue exceeds $30,000 CAD in a single quarter or over four quarters (e.g., July 1, 2024–June 30, 2025).



Voluntary: Register early to claim ITCs on startup costs (e.g., powder, tires, equipment), even if revenue is below $30,000. This is recommended for RiftMetro Powders to offset initial expenses.



Action:





Apply for HST Account:





Online: Use the BRO portal after getting your BN. Your HST number will be your BN plus “RT0001” (e.g., 987654321RT0001).



Phone: Call 1-800-959-5525.



Mail: Submit Form RC1 with HST account request.



Required: BN, business name, address, estimated annual revenue.



Set Up Filing Period: Choose monthly, quarterly, or annual HST filing based on revenue (annual for small businesses).



Charge HST: Include 13% HST on invoices for tire services and powder coating. Display your HST number on invoices (e.g., “HST# 987654321RT0001”).



Track ITCs: Record HST paid on business expenses (e.g., $1,500 CAD HST on a $10,000 oven) to deduct from HST collected when filing returns.

Step 5: Open a Business Bank Account with TD





Why? A separate business account keeps personal and business finances distinct, simplifying bookkeeping and tax filing. It’s also required for professional invoicing and CRA compliance.



Action:





Choose Account: Visit TD Canada Trust’s website or branch to select a business account (e.g., TD Basic Business Account, ~$5/month). Compare fees and features (e.g., online banking, transaction limits).



Gather Documents:





Business Name Registration (BIN and company key from Step 2).



Personal ID (e.g., driver’s license, passport).



BN from CRA (Step 3).



If incorporating, Articles of Incorporation and Certificate of Status.



Visit TD Branch or Apply Online: Provide documents and initial deposit (varies by account, ~$500 CAD).



Set Up Payments: Link the account for client payments (e.g., e-Transfers, debit/credit processing).



Note: A business plan isn’t required for a bank account but is essential for loans. Your polished business plan (from the previous artifact) is ready if you pursue financing.

Step 6: Set Up Bookkeeping for Tax Filing





Why? Proper bookkeeping tracks revenue, expenses, and HST for CRA filings and ensures you claim ITCs.



Action:





Choose a System: Start with a spreadsheet (e.g., Google Sheets) for simplicity, as outlined in the previous artifact’s bookkeeping template.



Track Transactions: Record all sales (tire services, powder coating), expenses (materials, rent), and HST collected/paid.



File HST Returns: Submit returns (monthly, quarterly, or annually) via CRA’s My Business Account. Report HST collected minus ITCs.



Prepare for Income Tax: As a sole proprietor, report business income on your personal tax return (T1). If incorporated, file a corporate tax return (T2).
